What is Tranquilism? It is a philosophy that proposes an alternative conception of value, where what matters is not to maximize desirable experiences, but to reach a state of being that is unruled by desire.

Here you can explore the foundations of the philosophy, read below, and follow along with the latest developments.


Essay:

The Architecture of Modern Scarcity

Reclaiming Our Peace in a Restless World

Introduction: You Aren't Failing. You're Just Exhausted.

If you have ever spent a quiet afternoon with your hands in the soil, coaxing something edible out of the earth, or created a beautiful poem or painting, you know exactly what natural, grounded abundance feels like. It is steady, patient, and deeply fulfilling. But at some point we all must wash our hands and step into the harsh noise of modern society’s rhythm.

We live in a world richer than any before it, yet we are collectively exhausted. If you are working hard, raising a family, or trying to build something meaningful, and still feel like you are constantly paddling just to keep your head above water, please take a deep breath. You are not failing. You are simply living in a system that may have begun in good faith but now depends on your exhaustion to keep itself going.

Think about what happens when you feel truly at peace. When you are deeply content, you don't feel a sudden, desperate urge to buy a new car to prove your worth. You don't need a barrage of subscriptions, impulse purchases, or hyper-palatable convenience foods to numb the stress of a grueling workday. A tranquil human being is a beautiful thing, but to an economy dependent on aggressive, infinite growth, a tranquil human being is a terrible consumer. The friction and stress you feel aren't personal defects; they are the inevitable consequence of the system’s design in order to maintain a growth percentage beyond what nature provides.

Part I: The Everyday Illusion

We are often told that if we just hustle a little harder and play by the rules, we will finally reach a plateau of abundance. With that abundance, safety and tranquillity. But the finish line keeps moving, and that friction became woven into our daily lives.

You see it playing out as we are all being gently nudged away from owning things that give us security, toward a world where we have to rent our own comfort—and our own survival—month by month. A good appliance gives out after a few short years, forcing you back to the store, because building things to last halts the consumption cycle. To build a thing that never needs to be replaced is poor business practice when valuing only expansion.

The screens of social media are becoming more prevalent and are expertly tuned to feed us a steady diet of unattainable lifestyles and outrage. At best, it is a constant stream of distortion designed to make people feel perpetually inadequate so that we will willingly buy the cure for the disease that it causes. At worst the algorithms become designed to sell you things that they are advertising while optimizing their advertisement strategy to alter your behavior.

For many, the paycheck is no longer a tool for building abundance; it is a ransom paid to avoid falling through the cracks. In 2016 half of Americans lived paycheck to paycheck today, in 2026 it has grown to 60%. The misery is spreading. How can we blame the individual in this situation? When basic human needs like healthcare and housing are used as leverage, it keeps us compliant and working longer hours, too tired to look up and question the rules of the game. Even worse, we begin to see those questioning the game as only trying to scam the current game. Thereby sealing our fate in this current charade as our "destiny"

Part II: The Squeeze on the Comfortable

Perhaps you feel insulated from this. Maybe you have a comfortable income, a home you love, and a healthy retirement account. If you haven't been battered by an economic downturn, it is easy to look at the struggles of others and think you have safely bypassed the storm.

But capital, by its very nature, consolidates. If our societal root value remains endless hoarding, the safety net will continue to shrink. 2016 it was half the country only ten years later in 2026 it’s 60% of the country. As the trend continues are we going to continue blaming the civilian? You can see that all day the mainstream media they are. “They’re addicted to their phones.” “They could afford a house if they didn’t buy avocado toast.”

Think of the next generation. The single-family homes and neighborhoods that previous generations took for granted are increasingly being purchased by corporate entities and landlords at a premium. By the time our children and grandchildren are ready to build their lives, they may find themselves permanently priced out, renting the very properties we once owned, and passing their wages upward to a consolidated class of owners. A group of owners that gets smaller and smaller every year as the apex capitalists push out those just enjoying their abundance. In a system that demands constant growth from finite resources, the comfortable middle class isn't permanently safe; it is simply the next resource in line to be harvested. Yes, the middle class is shrinking and it has nothing to do with people becoming more selfish or less intelligent. Actually quite the opposite, people have been getting smarter and those who realize that leisure makes one fall behind will come to dominate those simply enjoying the basics of being alive. It does not have to be like this, we do not need to force our children to become better and better strategists of the current system in order to merely survive or have an enjoyable life. Obviously individuals also must take inventory of what truly brings them happiness. Society cannot force people into introspection but it CAN make it impossible for them. The struggle for more is not worth it if you already have enough.

Part III: The Trap at the Top

It is easy to look at those at the very top of this system and see villains, but the reality is more tragic: they are just caught in a different kind of mental trap.

When the only organizing principle in life is to accumulate more, there is never a baseline of "enough." Hence the bliss of tranquility is never truly experienced. Just a bunch of ego strokes that never bring true fulfillment. Compiling systemic resources beyond what any human could reasonably use doesn't elevate a person's life; it breeds paranoia. When you hoard the resources others need to live, you must build higher walls, heavier doors, and fiercer defenses to protect your hoard.

In trading human connection and true tranquility for endless accumulation, the architects of this system have built themselves a gilded cage. They, too, have lost their peace trying to maintain it. It is systemic in nature because the systems measure of value is one thing: capital. The more you have the easier you have it. The most irrational and ironic part of being wealthy is that it is dramatically cheaper to be wealthy than it is to be poor. Wealthy individuals are constantly being given gifts in order to win economic opportunities from them. The wealthy can borrow money for much cheaper than a poor person. There are countless wealthy people who have no idea why they are getting more tax cuts but are happy to take the money. Much of the wealthy today see the problem in front of them when billionaires are pushing millionaires out of wealth. Yes, no amount of wealth can permanently buffer you or your future descendants from being pushed out of wealth by bigger fish. In a game that rewards the dominant it’s inevitable especially when we built an economy that crashes if the centers of outrage and inadequacy stop turning. You may be impervious to the tricks we play on ourselves but will your child or your great great grandchild? The value system of our society needs to change to keep the future unborn safe from this dark possibility.

Part IV: Tuning Out the Distortion

Careful realistic analysis of the atrocity that is our modern society can bring one to feeling betrayed by society and that betrayal hurts. Recognizing this is not about becoming angry; it is about shifting our perspective. It is about tuning out the harsh, artificial noise of the modern market and finding a more natural rhythm. When we realize that our struggles are not personal shortcomings, we stop blaming ourselves. We can step off the treadmill, look at the people around us, and begin the quiet rebellion of valuing connection, security, and human tranquility over relentless accumulation and domination. There are a lot of corrupted people today. They are not your enemies but, when they show their corruption, move on from them and keep moving until you find the people who have awakened to this. People who are valuing connection, community and truth.

Part V: The Tranquilist Manifesto

There is a third path beyond capitalism, socialism, and communism that is being defined: Tranquilism. Its ideology is a promise that all people make to one another: that every soul deserves a "floor of enough," and that a life well-lived is measured by peaceful tranquility, not by the endless accumulation of excess.

The Seven Axioms of Tranquilist Theory
  1. Tranquility is the terminal good: Growth, power, and wealth are only valuable if they contribute to the settled well-being of minds and bodies.
  2. Universal capacity: Every human has an equal claim to the conditions of calm, regardless of class, creed, or ability.
  3. Inner and outer conditions: The state cannot meditate for you, but it can make tranquility nearly impossible through precarity and fear. We must build a politics of conditions.
  4. Disturbance is the harm: We must audit every law, firm, and technology by one standard: What does this do to the tranquility of those it touches?.
  5. Sufficiency over maximization: Enough is a real quantity. Past a certain point, hoarding converts into domination, which is a disturbance to the social body.
  6. Universalizability: Tranquility purchased by another’s misery is not peace; it is merely comfort. We do not accept gated calm built on outsourced suffering.
  7. Means must resemble ends: We cannot build a tranquil world through terror or force. We commit to gradual, consensual, and experimental changes.

Part VI: Why Tranquility?

The concept has been developed over centuries and the foundation of its meaning has been established. We use that concept as the starting point for understanding what a functioning society (and human being) would look like. One where all the needs are being met. One where people do not have to compete for survival but make a commitment to understanding themselves and protecting each other. Understanding that all people on the planet are one complex organism interconnected by the air we breathe and the water we drink to the environment we live in, really helps us realize the need for a Tranquilist system. One where the values of the system do not contradict the values that we hold in our personal lives. Ensuring that our governmental institutions guarantee every human has the right to a tranquil environment is a huge win for our humanity. The effort is worthwhile because it is a blueprint for coexistence which is essential for achieving a universal enjoyable experience for everyone. Imagine not having to worry. Take a moment to think about what you feel your role would be in creating that world. Imagine how different things would be if we actually achieved an organized society that guaranteed that you have a place in building and maintaining the tranquility that we have achieved together through cooperation and trust. Imagine a world that doesn’t extract more than it can sustain. Imagine a world where outrage no longer sells. A world where we start seeing the enemies of tranquility for what they are, anti-human, anti-family and anti-heritage. We can have this world, we only need to change our idea of value.

Conclusion: The World We Can Create

The future belongs to the quiet. We possess the technological capacity to feed, house, and educate every person on this planet. The only thing standing between us and a post-scarcity reality is the outdated philosophy of growth-at-any-cost.

It is time to demote capitalism from the end-goal of our civilization to just a tool we use, keep it at the floor, quiet it, and let it serve us. The door to a tranquil world was never bolted shut. We simply have to make the choice to stop fighting over the crumbs and start paying attention to the people trying to build peace on earth for all humanity. Help us establish a new ideological foundation for the future of society for our species.
